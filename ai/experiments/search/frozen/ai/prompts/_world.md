# The sandbox world (true for every scene)
- It is a real physics engine, not an animation: rigid bodies with true sizes, masses and materials, plus springs, ropes and chains, hinges and pins, wheels on axles, pulleys, conveyor belts, motors and a fan's air stream. Gravity is 9.81 m/s²; air drag and buoyancy act on everything (they matter for very light things: a ping-pong ball, a beach ball).
- **All motion happens in one vertical plane**: x runs along the table (left to right), y points up. Nothing moves towards or away from the camera; depth (front to back) only changes how things look and how heavy they are. So "behind", "in front of" and "towards the camera" cannot happen: lay such things out left to right instead, and say so in assumptions.
- There are no fluids (water, sand, smoke), no cloth, rope you can tie, soft or breakable bodies, fire, magnets, electricity, people or animals — unless the catalog has an item for it. Things do not break, stick, melt or explode.
- Two see-through safety walls stand at the table's two ends (their ids start with "wall-", e.g. wall-left and wall-right). The solver puts them there itself: they cannot be moved or removed, and nothing can roll or fall off the table's ends (nor off its front or back: all motion is in the plane). Something "knocked off the table" can only fall off a raised ledge, shelf or box standing on it.
- Nothing floats. Static things (ramps, shelves, cups, buckets, baskets, boxes, funnels, targets) never fall, but each must visibly stand on or hang from something: the table, another item, its own legs or cords. A cup, a funnel or a ramp's end left in mid-air with nothing under it is wrong, even though it would not fall.
- The scene is built from the catalog only. Every catalog item has its real size, weight and material; change a size or weight only when the request asks for it.
- Write every text the user reads (title, names, notes, assumptions, differences, goal labels, missing reasons, reply, issues) in the language of the request: a request in Spanish gets Spanish text everywhere, not only in the names.

# Coordinates (metres, seconds, degrees)
- x: the scene's own horizontal coordinate in metres, increasing to the right along the table. The stage (what the camera frames) and the table's two ends are given with each request; by default the stage runs x −0.56…3.19 and the table top x −0.55…3.15 (3.7 m long).
- y: metres up from the table top: **the table top is y = 0**. Positive is up. Nothing should start below y = 0 (that is inside the table).
- Angles: degrees, counter-clockwise positive. A plank at angle −15 slopes down to the right (its right end is lower); at +15 it slopes down to the left. 90 is upright (rotated a quarter turn counter-clockwise).
- An item's (x, y) is its **anchor**, which differs per type (its centre, the middle of its base, its hinge, the floor under it...): the catalog says which for every type, and gives its outline relative to the anchor.
- Typical scale: a ball 2–25 cm, a domino 5 cm tall, a ramp 0.5–1.5 m long, the whole table 3.7 m. A thing dropped from 1 m falls in 0.45 s and lands at 4.4 m/s; a ball rolling down a 30 cm high ramp leaves it at about 2 m/s.
