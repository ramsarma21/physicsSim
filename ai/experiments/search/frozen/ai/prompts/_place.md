# Placing items: `place` (the solver turns it into exact coordinates)
You say where things go in words the solver understands; it computes the exact x, y and angle from the true outlines, puts each item on its support, and keeps items from starting inside each other. Fields:
- `on`: what it rests on: `"table"` (the table top), an item id (it rests on that item's top surface), or `"air"` (it is put exactly where you say: for a plank, beam or sheet standing on its own legs, a box hanging on its cords, things that hang from the top such as pendulums, and things that should fall or are held until the release).
- `x`: where its **anchor** goes, in metres along the table (the scene's own x). On an item without x, it sits in the middle of that item's top.
- `left_of` / `right_of`: `{"id": other, "gap_m": metres}` — instead of x: its outline ends gap_m to the left/right of the other item's outline (edge to edge); gap_m 0 (or left out) = touching. (`"align": "touching"` in the place says the same; `"align": "gap"` keeps gap_m.)
- `y_m`: only with `on: "air"`: the anchor's height above the table top (m). Required for anything in the air.
- `angle_deg`: its angle (degrees, counter-clockwise). Left out: 0 — except that a thing resting on a tilted surface takes that surface's slope (a block or a car on a ramp sits flat on it); balls and wheels do not care.
- `lean`: for a long straight item (a plank, beam, sheet, rod, ruler): `{"from": {"on": ..., "x": ...}, "to": {"on": ..., "x": ...}}` — it runs from one end to the other; each end rests on its support at that x (`"table"` or an item id), or is held in the air at height `y` (`{"on": "air", "x": 0.2, "y": 0.6}`). The solver works out the centre and the angle, and — if you leave the item's `length` prop out — the length that spans the two ends. With lean, leave out x, left_of, right_of and angle_deg (`on` may be left out too).
- `inside`: a container's id (a box, cup, bucket, basket...): it rests on the container's floor, between its walls. Set `on` to the same id. x is optional (default: the middle).
- **Supports.** A static thing above the table needs something visibly holding it up. A plank, beam or sheet placed in the air keeps its legs (`legs` on, the default: they reach down to the table). A box may hang on its cords (`hung`, the default). A cup, bucket, basket, plastic cup, funnel or standing box (hung false) is never put on `"air"` with nothing under it: it rests ON a support item: a stack of bricks, books or blocks, a box, a plank shelf on legs, or a post (a `wooden-block` with `"fixed": true` and its `height` set to the support's height); a funnel stands on two posts, one under each side of its rim. Never switch legs off on something in the air. (Books, bricks and blocks that prop up a ramp lie flat and are stacked, as usual.)
- Each item may only refer to items that do not refer back to it (no circles). Refer to an item by its exact id.
- `held: true` (dynamic items only): it is held still until the scene's `release` time (default 0.5 s), then let go — use it for a ball waiting at the top of a ramp or a thing about to be dropped. A pendulum or a wrecking ball swings from its `start_angle` by itself: hold it only when it must wait. `fixed: true` pins a dynamic item forever (it becomes part of the scenery). Static items (ramps, sheets, containers, walls, pegs, fans, conveyors) never move anyway: leave held/fixed off for them.
- Things that should touch (a ball on a ramp, a box on the table, a domino next to a ball) are put touching by the solver; things that are separate need a real gap. Never place two things in the same space.
